7. WHY NON-COOPERATIVE SEMANTICS BREAK THE MODEL (June 24 Update)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Attempting to make processes non-cooperative (where `my_f_coopt` = false, and the
override must explicitly send `true` to the old process to turn it off) breaks
`model.v` at a fundamental mathematical level:

In `model.v`, `reduceO` semantics explicitly forbid a `swi true` process from 
outputting `None` (`reduce_swiO` ONLY matches `swi false`). 
In our non-cooperative pipeline, because of the 1-cycle lag in the override 
mechanism, there is exactly one cycle where BOTH the old process (which just 
received `true` to turn off) and the new process (which just received `true` to 
turn on) are `swi true`. 

Since `model.v` forces any `swi true` process to output `Some o`, BOTH processes
attempt to output in the same cycle. This creates a bus collision in the 
`my_f_route_good` priority encoder, silently dropping the output of the 
lower-priority process. This violates the `Trace` structure (which expects single, 
sequential outputs like `out2 NOP` or `out0 (1, false)`) and causes the trace
derivation to fail completely with unification errors.

The solution is the one identified in Section 1(e): `my_f_coopt n := true`.
By making processes cooperative, they self-disable after one cycle, preventing 
overlap. The scheduler provides a continuous "level-triggered" enable by sending
`true` ONLY to the target process every cycle, naturally recreating continuous 
execution without pipeline collisions.
