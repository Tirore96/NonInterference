\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Non-interference} Non-interference goes back to Goguen and
Meseguer~\cite{goguen1982}; Sabelfeld and Myers survey language-based
approaches~\cite{sabelfeld2003}. Timing channels in programs have been closed by
padding transformations that equalise the running time of secret-dependent
branches~\cite{agat2000}; the sliced policy applies the same idea to handler
runs, with the NOP handler as padding.

\paragraph{Concurrency and scheduling} How the scheduler affects the security of
multi-threaded programs has been studied since Smith and Volpano~\cite{smith1998}.
Sabelfeld and Sands give a probabilistic, timing-sensitive non-interference for
multi-threaded programs~\cite{sabelfeld2000}, Mantel and Sudbrock a notion of
security that holds for a whole class of schedulers~\cite{mantel2010}, and Stefan et
al.\ address termination and timing channels in a concurrent information-flow
system~\cite{stefan2012}. These works treat the scheduler as given and ask which
programs are secure under it. We treat the interrupt mechanism, which decides when
handlers preempt the scheduled process, as the object to be made secure, for any
non-interfering scheduler and user processes.

\paragraph{Timing-channel mitigation} Predictive mitigation delays outputs to a
schedule predicted from public information, bounding what their timing
leaks~\cite{askarov2010}, also in a language-based setting~\cite{zhang2012}. Our
requirement has the same shape, applied to the interrupt mechanism: when secret
handlers run, and for how long, must be fixed by public events. The sliced policy
meets it exactly, so nothing leaks, at the cost of reserving handler time whether or
not it is used.

\paragraph{Composable timing-sensitive non-interference} Our proofs rest on the
calculus of Rafnsson et al.~\cite{rafnsson2017}, whose definitions are
timing-sensitive by construction and whose theorems compose. We mechanise the
calculus and its composition theorems, and apply them to a model of interrupt
handling.

\paragraph{Verified operating systems} Murray et al.\ formulate non-interference for
operating system kernels~\cite{murray2012} and prove it for seL4~\cite{murray2013},
whose proof leaves timing channels out of scope; Cock et al.\ measure the timing
channels that remain in seL4 and their mitigation~\cite{cock2014}. Time
protection~\cite{ge2019} argues that an operating system must partition time as well
as space, and includes the partitioning of interrupts among its
mechanisms\todo{check this characterisation against~\cite{ge2019}}; our sliced policy
is an instance of that idea in a small model. What we add is a timing-sensitive,
compositional, mechanised proof that the mechanism is secure, for any
non-interfering scheduler and user processes.
