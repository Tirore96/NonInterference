\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Non-interference} Non-interference goes back to Goguen and
Meseguer~\cite{goguen1982}; Sabelfeld and Myers survey language-based
approaches~\cite{sabelfeld2003}. Timing channels in programs have been closed by
padding transformations that equalise the running time of secret-dependent
branches~\cite{agat2000}; the sliced policy applies the same idea to handler
runs, with the NOP handler as padding.

\paragraph{Concurrency and scheduling} How the scheduler affects the security of
multi-threaded programs has been studied since Smith and Volpano~\cite{smith1998},
with probabilistic~\cite{sabelfeld2000}, scheduler-independent~\cite{mantel2010},
concurrent information-flow~\cite{stefan2012} and mechanised~\cite{vassena2017}
results. These works treat the scheduler as given and ask which
programs are secure under it. We treat the interrupt mechanism, which decides when
handlers preempt the scheduled process, as the object to be made secure.

\paragraph{Timing-channel mitigation} Predictive mitigation delays outputs to a
schedule predicted from public information, bounding what their timing
leaks~\cite{askarov2010}, also in a language-based setting~\cite{zhang2012}. Our
requirement has the same shape: when secret handlers run, and for how long, must be
fixed by public events. The sliced policy meets it exactly, at the cost of reserving
handler time whether or not it is used.

\paragraph{Composable timing-sensitive non-interference} Our proofs rest on the
calculus of Rafnsson et al.~\cite{rafnsson2017}, which we mechanise and apply to a
model of interrupt handling. Rafnsson and Sabelfeld investigate how information-flow
security composes in interactive systems~\cite{rafnsson2014}.

\paragraph{Verified operating systems} Murray et al.\ prove non-interference for
seL4~\cite{murray2012,murray2013}, leaving timing channels out of scope; Cock et al.\
measure those that remain~\cite{cock2014}. Time
protection~\cite{ge2019} argues that an operating system must partition time as well
as space, and includes the partitioning of interrupts among its
mechanisms\todo{check this characterisation against~\cite{ge2019}}; our sliced policy
is an instance of that idea in a small model, with a timing-sensitive, compositional,
mechanised proof. At the hardware interface, Zagieboylo et
al.\ design an instruction set that controls timing channels and leave exceptions and
asynchrony outside their formal proof~\cite{zagieboylo2019}.
