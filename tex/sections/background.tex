\section{Background: Composable Timing-Sensitive Non-Interference}\label{sec:background}

We recall the calculus of Rafnsson et al.~\cite{rafnsson2017}, with one change and
two additions. Non-interference is stated over finite traces, which the mechanisation
proves equivalent to the original coinductive definition (\appx). We add sum types and
a composition theorem for state cells under an invariant (\cref{thm:sta-pred}).

\subsection{Processes, traces and time}

A process $p : \Proc{I}{O}$ has input type $I$ and output type $O$. Types are
built from the empty type, the unit type $1$, booleans and naturals by
products $X \times Y$ and sums $X + Y$ (\m{Ty}); we write $A + 1$ for the optional
values of $A$, with injections \code{Some} and \code{None}. A process moves by
receiving an input, $\stepI{p}{i}{p'}$, or by emitting an output,
$\stepO{p}{o}{p'}$, in any order. Processes are built from seven constructors, whose
reduction rules are in \appx; our model is one closed term over them.

The simplest constructor, \code{out o}, ignores its input and always emits \code{o}. \code{map f g p} translates
inputs by \code{f} on the way into \code{p} and outputs by \code{g} on the way out.
\code{sta f g v p} keeps a state cell \code{v}: an input \code{i} updates it to
\code{f i v}, which \code{p} receives together with \code{i}; an output \code{o} of
\code{p} updates it to \code{g o v}, which is emitted together with \code{o}.
\code{swi b p} switches \code{p} on and off. While the phase \code{b} is
\code{false} it emits \code{None} and \code{p} takes no output step; while it is
\code{true} it emits \code{Some o} for an output \code{(c,o)} of \code{p}. The tag
\code{c} and the bit carried on each input toggle the phase. \code{par p1 p2} gives every input to
both parts and emits both outputs as a pair. \code{loop p} feeds each output of
\code{p} back to it as an input. \code{maybe p} makes the input optional: \code{None}
leaves \code{p} unchanged. Reduction is deterministic
(\m{reduceI_det}, \m{reduceO_det}), every process is input-total, accepting every input (\m{Proc_has_input}), and a
process never blocks on output (\m{Proc_has_output}).

Time is discrete, and transitions are conflated with time, as in prior
work~\cite{focardi2003,askarov2010,devriese2010,kashyap2011,rafnsson2017}. What is
timed is the work a process does, which is producing output, since inputs arrive
asynchronously: each output step takes one unit of time, and input steps take none,
arriving between outputs.

\subsection{Characterised equivalences}
Security is relative to levels $\ell$, partially ordered by $\sqsubseteq$ with a
least element, which is all the mechanisation assumes; a lattice, as usual in
the literature~\cite{denning1976}, is a special case. For our model the two-point lattice
$\Lo \sqsubseteq \Hi$ suffices: $\Lo$ is the attacker's level, and an observer at
$\Hi$ sees everything. What each level sees of a value is fixed by the
characterised equivalence of its type.

\begin{definition}[Characterised equivalence, \m{cEquiv}]\label{def:cequiv}
A \emph{characterised equivalence} $\eqv{A}$ on $A$ gives, for each level $\ell$, an
equivalence $\eqv{A}_\ell$ on $A$ (\code{rel}) and a predicate $\dis^A_\ell$ on
$A$ (\code{dis}) such that, for all $\ell \sqsubseteq \ell'$ and $x, y \in A$,
\begin{gather*}
x \eqv{A}_{\ell'} y \Rightarrow x \eqv{A}_\ell y, \qquad
\dis^A_{\ell'}\,x \Rightarrow \dis^A_\ell\,x, \\
\dis^A_\ell\,x \Rightarrow (\dis^A_\ell\,y \iff x \eqv{A}_\ell y).
\end{gather*}
\end{definition}

An observer at $\ell$ cannot tell $x$ from $y$ if $x \eqv{A}_\ell y$, and does not
see $x$ at all if $\dis^A_\ell\,x$. Rafnsson et al.\ instead extend each type $A$
with a value $\unobs$, to $A_\unobs$, and write $x \eqv{A}_\ell \unobs$ for
$\dis^A_\ell\,x$. The predicate was more convenient to mechanise, and the two give
the same non-interference (\m{NI_lEquiv}).

The equivalences of our model are built from base equivalences with formers. We say
$\eqv{A}$ \emph{hides} $x$ at $\ell$, and $x$ is \emph{unobservable} at $\ell$, when
$\dis^A_\ell\,x$, and write the predicate
of a built equivalence with the same superscript as its relation.

\begin{definition}[Base equivalence, \m{base_equiv}]\label{def:base}
For a set $K$ of levels closed upwards ($\ell \in K \wedge \ell \sqsubseteq \ell'
\Rightarrow \ell' \in K$), $\doteq^K$ on a type $A$ is given by, for all $\ell$ and
$x, x' \in A$,
\[
x \doteq^K_\ell x' \iff x = x' \vee \ell \notin K, \qquad
\dis^K_\ell\,x \iff \ell \notin K.
\]
\end{definition}

Values are related if they are equal or hidden, and they are hidden at levels outside $K$. We write $\doteq^\ell$ for $\doteq^K$ with
$K = \{\ell' \mid \ell \sqsubseteq \ell'\}$. The \emph{public} equivalence $\pubeq$ thus has $K = \{\Lo, \Hi\}$ and hides
nothing (\m{public_equiv}). The \emph{private} equivalence $\priveq$ has
$K = \{\Hi\}$ (\m{private_equiv}): at $\Lo$ it relates and hides all values,
and at $\Hi$ it is equality.

Equivalences on pairs and sums follow the type constructors: pairs are related
componentwise and sums tag by tag, and otherwise only when both are hidden.
The three formers of each kind differ only in when a value is hidden.

\begin{definition}[Products]\label{def:formers}
Let $\eqv{X}$ and $\eqv{Y}$ be equivalences on $X$ and $Y$. For
${\otimes} \in \{{\times}, {\times_R}, {\times_{LR}}\}$ (\m{eqpair}, \m{eqpair_R},
\m{eqpair_LR}), $\eqo{X} \otimes \eqo{Y}$ on $X \times Y$ is given by
\begin{gather*}
(x,y) \mathrel{\eqo{X}_\ell \otimes \eqo{Y}_\ell} (x',y') \\
\iff \\
x \eqv{X}_\ell x' \wedge y \eqv{Y}_\ell y' \vee
  \dis^{X \otimes Y}_\ell\,(x,y) \wedge \dis^{X \otimes Y}_\ell\,(x',y'),
\end{gather*}
where
\begin{align*}
\dis^{X \times Y}_\ell\,(x,y) &\iff \mathsf{false}, \\
\dis^{X \times_R Y}_\ell\,(x,y) &\iff \dis^Y_\ell\,y, \\
\dis^{X \times_{LR} Y}_\ell\,(x,y) &\iff \dis^X_\ell\,x \wedge \dis^Y_\ell\,y.
\end{align*}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Sums]\label{def:sum}
Let $\eqv{X}$ and $\eqv{Y}$ be equivalences on $X$ and $Y$. For
${\oplus} \in \{{+}, {+_R}, {+_{LR}}\}$ (\m{eqsum}, \m{eqsum_R}, \m{eqsum_LR}),
$\eqo{X} \oplus \eqo{Y}$ on $X + Y$ is given by
\begin{align*}
\mathsf{inl}\,x \mathrel{\eqo{X}_\ell \oplus \eqo{Y}_\ell} \mathsf{inl}\,x' &\iff x \eqv{X}_\ell x', \\
\mathsf{inr}\,y \mathrel{\eqo{X}_\ell \oplus \eqo{Y}_\ell} \mathsf{inr}\,y' &\iff y \eqv{Y}_\ell y', \\
\mathsf{inl}\,x \mathrel{\eqo{X}_\ell \oplus \eqo{Y}_\ell} \mathsf{inr}\,y &\iff
  \dis^{X \oplus Y}_\ell\,(\mathsf{inl}\,x) \wedge \dis^{X \oplus Y}_\ell\,(\mathsf{inr}\,y),
\end{align*}
and symmetrically, where
\begin{align*}
\dis^{X + Y}_\ell\,z &\iff \mathsf{false}, \\
\dis^{X +_R Y}_\ell\,z &\iff \exists y.\; z = \mathsf{inr}\,y \wedge \dis^Y_\ell\,y, \\
\dis^{X +_{LR} Y}_\ell\,z &\iff \exists x.\; z = \mathsf{inl}\,x \wedge \dis^X_\ell\,x \\
&\phantom{{}\iff{}} \vee \exists y.\; z = \mathsf{inr}\,y \wedge \dis^Y_\ell\,y.
\end{align*}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Non-interference}

Non-interference is stated over the traces a process performs.

\begin{definition}[Traces, \m{Trace}]\label{def:trace}
A \emph{trace} $t$ over $I$ and $O$ is a finite word of input effects $?i$ with
$i \in I$ and output effects $!o$ with $o \in O$; $\epsilon$ is the empty trace and
``$.$'' concatenation. A process $p : \Proc{I}{O}$ \emph{performs} $t$ at $\ell$,
written $\perf{p}{t}{\ell}$, as given by the rules
\begin{mathpar}
\inferrule{ }{\perf{p}{\epsilon}{\ell}}
\and
\inferrule{\stepI{p}{i}{p'} \\ \perf{p'}{t}{\ell}}{\perf{p}{?i.t}{\ell}}
\and
\inferrule{\stepO{p}{o'}{p'} \\ o' \eqv{O}_\ell o \\ \perf{p'}{t}{\ell}}
  {\perf{p}{!o.t}{\ell}}
\end{mathpar}
\end{definition}

A process thus performs $t$ if it has a run with the effects of $t$, outputs
compared up to $\eqv{O}_\ell$.

\begin{definition}[Non-interference, \m{NI}]\label{def:ni}
$p \in \NI(\eqv{I}, \eqv{O})$ iff for all $\ell$, $t$, $t'$, $i$ and $i'$:
\begin{align*}
i \eqv{I}_\ell i' &\;\Rightarrow\; \bigl(\perf{p}{t.?i.t'}{\ell} \Rightarrow
  \perf{p}{t.?i'.t'}{\ell}\bigr), \\
\dis^I_\ell\,i &\;\Rightarrow\; \bigl(\perf{p}{t.t'}{\ell} \Leftrightarrow
  \perf{p}{t.?i.t'}{\ell}\bigr).
\end{align*}
\end{definition}

The first clause (substitution) makes related inputs interchangeable. The second
lets an input $i$ with $\dis^I_\ell\,i$ be inserted into a trace (insertion,
$\Rightarrow$) or deleted from it (deletion, $\Leftarrow$) without the observer
noticing.

\subsection{Compositional theorems}\label{sec:composition}

The calculus has one non-interference theorem per constructor. We write
$\eqv{V}$ for an equivalence on the state of a \code{sta} and $\eqv{B}$ for one on the
\emph{gate bits} of a \code{swi}, the tag and the input bit that toggle its phase.

\begin{definition}[Side conditions]\label{def:side}
For all equivalences $\eqv{X}$, $\eqv{Y}$ and $\eqv{V}$, functions $f$ and processes
$p$ with output type $Y$, and, in the lines of \code{swi}, all $x \in X$ and levels
$\ell$, the side conditions of \code{map}, \code{sta} and \code{swi} are
\begin{alignat*}{2}
&\text{\small\code{map}}\; & f \in \NI(\eqv{X},\eqv{Y}) &\iff \forall \ell, x, x'.\;
  x \eqv{X}_\ell x' \Rightarrow f\,x \eqv{Y}_\ell f\,x' \\
&& f \in \mathsf{PU}(\eqv{X},\eqv{Y}) &\iff \forall \ell, x.\;
  \dis^X_\ell\,x \Rightarrow \dis^Y_\ell\,(f\,x) \\[3pt]
&\text{\small\code{sta}} & f \in \NI(\eqv{X},\eqv{V},\eqv{V}) &\iff \forall \ell, x, x', v, v'.\;
  x \eqv{X}_\ell x' \wedge v \eqv{V}_\ell v' \\
&&  &\hphantom{{}\iff{}} \Rightarrow f\,x\,v \eqv{V}_\ell f\,x'\,v' \\
&& f \in \mathsf{PE}(\eqv{X},\eqv{V}) &\iff \forall \ell, x, v.\;
  \dis^X_\ell\,x \Rightarrow f\,x\,v \eqv{V}_\ell v \\[3pt]
&\text{\small\code{swi}} & \ell \in \Aw(x,\eqv{X}) &\iff \forall x'.\; x \eqv{X}_\ell x' \\
&&  &\hphantom{{}\iff{}} \Rightarrow x = x' \wedge \neg\dis^X_\ell\,x' \\
&& \ell \in \Ob(p,\eqv{Y}) &\iff \forall t.\; \perf{p}{t}{\ell} \\
&&  &\hphantom{{}\iff{}} \Rightarrow \dis^Y_\ell\,y \text{ for every $!y$ in $t$}
\end{alignat*}
\end{definition}

A function is non-interfering if it maps related values to related ones
(\m{f_NI}, \m{fv_NI}), unobservable-preserving if it maps unobservable values to
unobservable ones (\m{f_PU}), and preserves equivalence if an unobservable input
does not visibly move the state (\m{f_EP}). An observer is \emph{aware} of $x$
(\m{aware}) if it can tell $x$ from every other value and sees it, and \emph{oblivious} to $p$
(\m{oblivious}) if it sees none of $p$'s outputs.

\begin{theorem}[Composition~\cite{rafnsson2017}]\label{thm:composition}
For all processes, functions and equivalences of matching types:
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{it:out} (\m{out_NI}) $\mathsf{out}\;o \in \NI(\eqv{I},\eqv{O})$.
\item\label{it:map} (\m{map_NI}) If $p \in \NI(\eqv{I'},\eqv{O})$,
  $f \in \NI(\eqv{I},\eqv{I'}) \cap \mathsf{PU}(\eqv{I},\eqv{I'})$ and
  $g \in \NI(\eqv{O},\eqv{O'})$, then
  $\mathsf{map}\;f\;g\;p \in \NI(\eqv{I},\eqv{O'})$.
\item\label{it:sta} (\m{sta_NI}) If $p \in \NI(\eqo{V} \times_R \eqo{I},\eqv{O})$,
  $f \in \NI(\eqv{I},\eqv{V},\eqv{V}) \cap \mathsf{PE}(\eqv{I},\eqv{V})$ and
  $g \in \NI(\eqv{O},\eqv{V},\eqv{V})$, then
  $\mathsf{sta}\;f\;g\;v\;p \in \NI(\eqv{I},\eqo{V} \times \eqo{O})$.
\item\label{it:swi} (\m{swi_NI}) If $p \in \NI(\eqv{I},\eqo{B} \times_{LR} \eqo{O})$ and
  for every $\ell$,
  $\ell \in \Aw(\mathsf{true},\eqv{B}) \cup
    \Ob(\mathsf{swi}\;b\;p,\eqo{O} +_{LR} \one^{\Aw(\mathsf{true},\eqv{B})})$, then
  $\mathsf{swi}\;b\;p \in
    \NI(\eqo{B} \times_{LR} \eqo{I},\eqo{O} +_{LR} \one^{\Aw(\mathsf{true},\eqv{B})})$.
\item\label{it:par} (\m{par_NI}) If $p_1 \in \NI(\eqv{I},\eqv{O_1})$ and
  $p_2 \in \NI(\eqv{I},\eqv{O_2})$, then
  $\mathsf{par}\;p_1\;p_2 \in \NI(\eqv{I},\eqo{O_1} \times \eqo{O_2})$.
\item\label{it:loop} (\m{loop_NI}) If $p \in \NI(\eqv{I},\eqv{I})$, then
  $\mathsf{loop}\;p \in \NI(\eqv{I},\eqv{I})$.
\item\label{it:maybe} (\m{maybe_NI}) If $p \in \NI(\eqv{I},\eqv{O})$, then
  $\mathsf{maybe}\;p \in \NI(\eqo{I} +_{LR} \one^\emptyset,\eqv{O})$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Two of these carry substantial side conditions: the state update of \code{sta} must
be non-interfering and equivalence-preserving, and \code{swi} requires every observer
to be aware of the gate or oblivious to the gated process.

The side conditions of \code{sta} are too strict for our model: they must hold on
every pair of related states, including states the process never reaches. We
therefore add a variant of \comp{it:sta} that asks for them only on states
satisfying an invariant $P$ that the state updates preserve.

\begin{definition}[Side conditions under an invariant, \m{fv_NI_on}, \m{f_EP_on}, \m{pres_on}]\label{def:side-pred}
For all equivalences $\eqv{X}$ and $\eqv{V}$, predicates $P$ on $V$ and functions
$f : X \to V \to V$,
\begin{align*}
f \in \NI_P(\eqv{X},\eqv{V},\eqv{V}) &\iff \forall \ell, x, x', v, v'.\;
  x \eqv{X}_\ell x' \wedge v \eqv{V}_\ell v' \\
  &\hphantom{{}\iff{}} \wedge P\,v \wedge P\,v' \Rightarrow f\,x\,v \eqv{V}_\ell f\,x'\,v' \\
f \in \mathsf{PE}_P(\eqv{X},\eqv{V}) &\iff \forall \ell, x, v.\;
  \dis^X_\ell\,x \wedge P\,v \Rightarrow f\,x\,v \eqv{V}_\ell v \\
f \in \mathsf{Pres}(P) &\iff \forall x, v.\; P\,v \Rightarrow P\,(f\,x\,v)
\end{align*}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Invariant composition, \m{sta_NI_pred}]\label{thm:sta-pred}
For all processes, functions, equivalences and predicates $P$ of matching types: if
$P\,v$, $f, g \in \mathsf{Pres}(P)$, $p \in \NI(\eqo{V} \times_R \eqo{I},\eqv{O})$,
$f \in \NI_P(\eqv{I},\eqv{V},\eqv{V}) \cap \mathsf{PE}_P(\eqv{I},\eqv{V})$ and
$g \in \NI_P(\eqv{O},\eqv{V},\eqv{V})$, then
$\mathsf{sta}\;f\;g\;v\;p \in \NI(\eqv{I},\eqo{V} \times \eqo{O})$.
\end{theorem}
