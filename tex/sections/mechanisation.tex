\section{Mechanisation}\label{sec:mechanisation}

The development is about 6{,}400 lines of Rocq 9.0.1, available at
\url{\repobase}, using Mathematical Components~2.5.0~\cite{mathcomp} with
Hierarchy Builder~1.10.1, deriving~0.2.2 and, for the coinductive definitions of
the original paper, paco~4.2.3~\cite{paco}. Continuous
integration rebuilds it on every change and checks that the paper's results
assume nothing beyond the two parameters that fix the security levels. Names of
definitions and theorems in typewriter font link to the sources.

\paragraph{Departures} Our definitions follow Rafnsson et al., with one essential
departure: the original defines non-interference coinductively over infinite streams,
and we quantify over finite traces (\cref{def:ni}), which makes every proof an
induction on a trace. \m{NI_paper} proves the two equivalent, so
\cref{thm:leak,thm:main} also hold as the original reads them. \Appx{} gives the
details and the development's files.

\paragraph{Scope} The development fixes the shape of the pool: six slots, with one
scheduler, two user processes and three handlers, and the two interrupts. Interrupts
are inputs. What causes a disk interrupt, such as the disk answering a request of the
private user process, is left to the environment, and \cref{thm:main} holds for every
sequence of interrupts, so for every such disk. The scheduler and the public user
process take no input, so a scheduler is a fixed sequence of choices: it cannot wake a
process when its interrupt arrives, a channel the model leaves out. The private user
process emits only private output, and all handlers have the same fixed length.
\Cref{thm:leak} is a counterexample on the concrete system. The listings are typed by hand
from the Rocq sources, and the runs in the figures are proved traces (\appx).
